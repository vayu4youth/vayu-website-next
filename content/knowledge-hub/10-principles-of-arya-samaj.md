Arya Samaj is a Hindu reform movement founded in Bombay in 1875 by Swami Dayanand Saraswati. The movement was created to revive what Dayanand understood as the original teaching of the Vedas and to encourage people to live according to truth, knowledge, righteousness, and social responsibility. The ten principles of Arya Samaj provide a framework for understanding God, seeking knowledge, accepting truth, acting ethically, and working for the welfare of society. Although these principles were established 150 years ago, many of their ideas still apply to modern issues such as misinformation and discrimination.

### Principle 1

> God is the original source of all that is true knowledge and all that is known by physical sciences.

This principle places knowledge at the foundation of human life. From the Arya Samaj perspective, knowledge is not something that should be ignored or taken for granted. People should seek to understand the world and distinguish genuine knowledge from ignorance. This principle can connect to education and critical thinking. Students have access to enormous amounts of information, but not all information is accurate. Applying this principle could mean researching a topic before blindly believing it.

### Principle 2

> God is existent, Conscious, All beautitude, Formless, Almighty, Just, Merciful, Unbegotten, Infinite, Unchangeable, Beginningless, Incomparable, the support of All, the Lord or all, All-pervading, Omniscient and Controller of All from within, Evermature, Imperishable, Fearless, Eternal, Pure and Creator of the universe. IT alone must be worshipped.

This principle presents justice and mercy as qualities associated with God. These qualities can also influence how a person thinks about their own behavior. It means trying to be fair while also being compassionate.

### Principle 3

> The Vedas are the books of all **TRUE** knowledge. It is the paramount duty of all Aryas to read them, to teach them to others, to listen to them, and to recite them to others.

By showing the importance Arya Samaj places on education, this principle reflects Dayanand's belief that people should have access to Vedic learning rather than simply accepting religious ideas without understanding them. This principle can be applied by learning about one's cultural and religious heritage.

### Principle 4

> All persons should always be ready to accept the truth and renounce the untruth.

This principle is especially relevant to the modern world because information can spread extremely quickly. A person can encounter a claim online and immediately share it without checking whether it is accurate or not. Applying this principle could mean checking sources before sharing information and admitting mistakes. It means being honest with your peers, family, and teachers.

### Principle 5

> All acts ought to be performed in conformity with dharma (Righteousness and Duty) i.e., after due consideration of the truth and the untruth.

The 5th principle states that actions should be performed according to Dharma, or righteousness and duty, after considering what is true and untrue and what is right and wrong. A person should not simply follow a crowd and should think before taking actions. This applies to social media, friendships, family decisions, and future careers. Asking ourselves what is right before making a decision is very important.

### Principle 6

> The primary object of the Arya Samaj is to do good to the whole world i.e. to promote physical, spiritual, and social progress of all humans.

This principle expands religion beyond an individual's personal relationship with God. It says that people should also care about the well-being of others. Community service and helping others can make this principle sustainable. The larger lesson is that individuals have responsibilities beyond themselves.

### Principle 7

> Your dealings with all should be regulated by love and due justice in accordance with the dictates of dharma (righteousness).

This principle encourages people to treat others with dignity and fairness. For today's generation, this can be applied to bullying, discrimination, and friendships. If someone is being excluded or mistreated, standing up for that person can be an example of justice.

### Principle 8

> Avidyaa (illusion and ignorance) is to be dispelled, and Vidyaa (realisation and acquisition of knowledge) should be promoted.

Knowledge is therefore not just something that benefits the individual. It is something that should be shared so that other people can benefit as well. In today's world, technology gives people access to information, but misinformation and misunderstandings can also spread quickly. Applying the principle could mean learning from reliable sources, questioning misinformation, and helping others understand important topics.

### Principle 9

> None should remain satisfied with one's own elevation only, but should incessantly strive for the social upliftment of all, realise one's own elevation in the elevation of others.

This principle states that people should not be satisfied with their own advancement but should work toward the social advancement of everyone. This challenges the idea that success is only about individual achievement. It doesn't mean people should give up their own goals; instead, personal success can become more meaningful when it contributes to the success of others.

### Principle 10

> All persons ought to dedicate themselves necessarily for the social good and the well-being of all, subordinating their personal interest, while the individual is free to enjoy freedom of action for the individual's well-being.

This principle recognizes that people need both freedom and responsibility. This is particularly relevant today because individuals have many ways to express themselves and make personal choices. People have to make choices while still considering how those choices might affect their families, communities, and environment.

---

The modern world has changed significantly since Arya Samaj was founded, but the problems of misinformation, ignorance, discrimination, selfishness, and social responsibility remain important. The ten principles connect to all these problems. This does not mean that every modern issue has an exact answer within the ten principles. Instead, the principles can provide a framework for thinking about modern problems. These principles were not developed as part of Swami Dayanand Saraswati's effort to return to Vedic teachings and promote religious and social reform. They emphasize truth, knowledge, righteousness, and justice. For today's generation. The principles can be understood as more than historical religious teachings. They can provide a way to think about how people use information, treat others, and make decisions. Ultimately, the ten principles emphasize a connection between self-improvement and the improvement of society.
