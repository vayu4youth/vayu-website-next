Yajña is one of the greatest acts in the universe. If I say that **all great things are, in some way, Yajña**, it would not be wrong. The Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa states:

> **"यज्ञो वै श्रेष्ठतमं कर्म"** (1.7.1.5)
>
> Yajña is indeed the most glorious work.

Yajña is also a way of worship and a way of giving. It is connected with the well-being and prosperity of life. Well Agni (fire) has a unique transformative power. Whatever is offered into it undergoes transformation, and its products can spread into the surrounding environment. That's great isn't it, and every great thing comes with great responsibility. If someone wants a healthy body, they should know what they are putting into it. **Knowing what we consume is necessary.**

In the same way, if we want to perform Yajña properly, we should know what ingredients we are putting into the Havan. Maharshi Dayanand ji, illustrator in sanskarvidhi, very simply divides the Ingredients used for homa into **four categories**.

### 1. Sustenance & Strength *(Pushtidayak)*

Cow Ghee, milk, wheat, rice, etc provide strength, which is why we put it. These ingredients, especially ghee, act as an excellent combustion agent that sustains the fire at an optimal temperature. Upon vaporization, it coats medicinal smoke particles, allowing them to diffuse broadly into the atmosphere.

### 2. Health & Healing *(Arogyadayak)*

All the herbs that we get from mother nature such as Giloy, Ashwagandha, Tulsi, Brahmi, Neem leaves, and Turmeric. That keeps us away from disease, and helps body and mind stay pain free

### 3. Sweetness & Joy *(Madhur)*

We all like some kind of sweetness in our life so this is very important. Honey, Jaggery, Raw Sugar, Dried Fruits, even we can make halva, and other sweets too are symbolic of sweetness in thoughts, speech, and provide a nice energy.

### 4. Fragrance & Peace *(Sugandhit)*

As humans we use resources from earth and keep getting this earth smelly and dirty, so we should use **Sandalwood**, Camphor, Guggul resin, Cardamom, Cloves, and other natural ingredients.

---

Not only these things but also all kinds of natural food, plants and herbs, all the good and appropriate things that are created by nature. Use all those nice things for havan, there is some name of ingredients and we can find in direct from veda:

> **व्रीहयश्च मे यवाश्च मे माषाश्च मे तिलाश्च मे मुद्गाश्च मे ....... यज्ञेन कल्पन्ताम् ॥ १२ ॥**

This cyclical relationship is expressed in the **Yajurveda (18.12)**, where various grains and crops are consecrated for collective welfare: Well it can also be possible that sometimes not having enough accessibility or regardless of financial means doesn't matter. If rare herbs or expensive ghee are unavailable, simple offerings of rice and raw sugar are fully sufficient. If physical materials are unavailable altogether, the focused recitation of Veda mantras alone carries profound spiritual efficacy.

Furthermore, we are not just using or taking from nature, we are trying to give back where it came from. Yajña operates in a continuous, natural cycle. The scriptures says:

> **"यज्ञकर्मणा जलवायुशुद्ध्या वृष्टिर्भवति, वृष्ट्यैव सर्वप्राणिभ्यः सुखं संपद्यते।"**

It means when offerings are made into the sacred fire, they purify the elements, which in turn brings clean rain. This rain nourishes the earth to yield healthy grains and brings joy for all living beings, and that crops or grain which are then used to sustain life and offer back into the *havan*. Some people called it science, some called it a miracle. As stewards of the planet, humans hold a direct duty to protect the surrounding environment. Performing Yajña correctly yields positive environmental benefits, whereas using improper or toxic materials can cause negative impacts.

Finally, when Yajña is performed with the recommended ingredients, it may help purify the surrounding environment, with some ingredients reported to have antimicrobial properties, while creating a fresh, fragrant atmosphere at home. As the fire burns, the ritual may also encourage a calmer, more meditative mood. Thus, Yajña is not only about the outer environment but also the inner environment. The mantras, like affirmations and prayers, add a deeper spiritual dimension, creating a sense of overall well-being and harmony.
